Scientific Publications · Insight

Authorship Criteria Under ICMJE, Explained

Who qualifies as an author, who doesn’t, and how to settle it before the first draft is written.

SciPrez Scientific Publications Team 6 min read Publication Practice
On This Page
Key Takeaways
  • Authorship requires meeting all four ICMJE criteria-not just funding, running, or supervising the study.
  • Contribution alone-collecting data, providing funding, general supervision-does not qualify someone as an author on its own.
  • Authorship is far easier to settle before drafting begins than to renegotiate after.
  • Contributors who don’t meet all four criteria should be acknowledged, not listed as authors.

Why authorship disputes keep happening

Authorship disputes are one of the most common-and most avoidable-sources of friction in multi-author manuscripts, particularly ones involving medical affairs teams, clinical investigators, and external key opinion leaders. The disputes rarely come from bad faith. They come from a shared but incorrect assumption: that meaningful involvement in a study automatically earns a byline.

The International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) defines authorship through four specific criteria, and all four must be met for someone to be listed as an author. Understanding them-and applying them early-prevents most of the disputes that otherwise surface at submission, when they’re hardest to resolve.

The four ICMJE criteria

Authorship is not a single bar to clear. It’s four separate ones, and a contributor needs to clear every one of them.

1 Substantial Contribution
2 Drafting or Critical Revision
3 Final Approval
4 Accountability
All Four Required

Meeting only one or two of these criteria qualifies a contributor for acknowledgment-not authorship.

01

Substantial contribution

Either to the conception and design of the work, or to the acquisition, analysis, or interpretation of the data. This is usually the criterion people assume is sufficient on its own. It isn’t.

02

Drafting or critical revision

Actually writing the manuscript, or critically revising it for important intellectual content. Light editorial comments or formatting suggestions don’t meet this bar.

03

Final approval

Reviewing and formally approving the final version submitted for publication-not a general awareness that the paper exists.

04

Accountability

Agreeing to be accountable for the accuracy and integrity of the entire work, not just the section they personally contributed to.

All four criteria must be met-not just one or two.

What doesn’t qualify someone as an author

This is where most disputes actually originate. A number of genuinely valuable contributions to a study don’t, on their own, meet the bar for authorship-and ICMJE is explicit that these should be acknowledged, not credited as authorship.

Qualifies as Authorship

  • Substantial design or data contribution
  • Drafting or critical revision of the manuscript
  • Final approval of the submitted version
  • Accountability for the work as a whole
- all four, together

Acknowledge Instead

  • Acquiring funding on its own
  • General supervision of the research group
  • Data collection without analytical input
  • Technical editing or language polishing
  • Providing patients or specimens only
